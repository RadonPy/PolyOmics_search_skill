# Output rules

Lead with the answer and the limits that affect the decision. For a shortlist, show UUIDs, structures or SMILES when relevant, simulated values with verified units, filters and quality criteria, ordering, duplicate handling, and dataset revision. Respect requests for just a value or a specific output format. Keep material unit, reliability and coverage limitations visible. Distinguish unreported values, zero matches and tool failure.

For each target, label the simulation value, exact Source prediction if legitimately available, exploratory experimental estimate, independently validated prediction, proxy, or hypothesis. Separate published observations and statistics from newly calculated values. Use Y units for an estimate and report the formula, coefficient units, fitted pair count, scope and optional fitted R². Cite the study and Evidence ID, Evidence release and revision, current MD revision, and known or unknown original MD revision. Share common provenance once for comparable rows. Report the candidate-specific conditions, domain cautions, and reasons a value cannot be computed or used for screening.

Identify the validation target and split. Report member counts, coverage and independence separately from independent Source MD assessment and any evaluation of the experimental mapping. Preserve property warnings on their stated variables. State when prediction uncertainty is unquantified. Uncorrected parity error, cross-property association error, a coefficient interval and member RMSE each describe a different quantity.

Explain what supports a screening decision, which criteria remain unresolved, and how modeling or domain limitations could change the ranking. For multiple constraints, use per-property results and transparent tradeoffs, or user-specified weighting. Avoid combining incompatible raw units or inventing a combined pass probability. Describe the candidate pool when search or paging limits prevent a global ranking.

For design, connect each proposed structural change or candidate to physical reasoning and available Evidence. Explain plausible competing mechanisms. For example, water uptake may depend on polymer–water interactions, free volume, morphology, and test conditions. Propose targeted measurements where they can distinguish hypotheses or resolve a required constraint. Treat conclusions from thin or conflicting support as tentative.
